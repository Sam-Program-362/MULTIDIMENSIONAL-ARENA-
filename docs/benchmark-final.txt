=== FINAL BENCHMARK — 40 seeds, 5400 tick cap (90s @ 60 t/s) ===
Game rules and tuning are the shipped Phase 1c.3 source (exposure active). No
rules or tuning were changed to produce this report.

Scenario (e) is reported in the ORIGINAL task direction: the goal is that a
scripted SKILLED COUNTER-PUNCHER wins at least 55% of its fights AGAINST the AI
(counter-puncher win rate >= 55%), NOT the AI. Two counter-puncher scripts were
built during development; both are reported side by side below. (The committed
harness scripts/benchmark.ts prints the same fight from the AI's side, i.e.
AI% = 100 - counter% here since there were no draws; only the reported
direction differs, not the fights.)

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
#  Scenario              Result                                      Goal (original)     Met?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
a  Stationary spammer    spammer wins 60.0% (AI 27.5%)               spammer <= 50%      NO
b  Idle player           AI wins 100.0%, draws 0, stalemates 0       AI >= 95%, 0 draw   YES
c  Holds Block forever   AI wins 97.5%                               AI >= 90%           YES
d  Runs away + Dodge     avg reach 172.1 ticks (2.87s), never 0      <= 180 ticks (3s)   YES
e  Counter-puncher       see the two scripts below                   counter >= 55%      see below
f  Two spammers          player 37.5% / dummy 57.5% / draw 2         each 35-65%         YES
g  AI vs AI              finished 40/40, avg 406 ticks               no stalemate        YES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Scenario (e) — counter-puncher, ORIGINAL wording (counter-puncher wins >= 55%):

  (1) Competent turtle-and-punish script
      Behavior: stays in range, keeps a standing guard by default, dodges to
      protect endurance and to bait whiffs, and drops in to punish every
      observed opening (recovery / stagger / Exposed AI), cashing the 1.75x
      multiplier on an Exposed AI.
      Result:  counter wins 100.0%  /  AI wins 0.0%  /  draws 0
      Goal counter >= 55%:  MET

  (2) guardBias 0.85 script (the later, deliberately weaker reactor)
      Behavior: same punishing/dodging, but only holds its guard on ~85% of
      no-read ticks (drops it the rest), so the AI's guard-break and Exposure
      punishes get through.
      Result:  counter wins 25.0%  /  AI wins 75.0%  /  draws 0
      Goal counter >= 55%:  NOT MET

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goals met under the ORIGINAL wording of every scenario:

  With counter-puncher script (1) turtle-and-punish for (e):
      met: b, c, d, e, f, g   ->  6 / 7   (only (a) not met)

  With counter-puncher script (2) guardBias 0.85 for (e):
      met: b, c, d, f, g      ->  5 / 7   (a) and (e) not met)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Notes:
- The original counter-puncher goal IS achievable: a competent, skilled
  turtle-and-punish counter-puncher beats the AI 100% of the time (script 1).
- The only scenario that cannot be met is (a): a cleanly connecting stationary
  spammer never self-exposes (landing hits is not punished by design) and the
  fixed Rookie attacks only ~5x per fight, so (a) floors at 60% (down from the
  82.5% baseline). Lowering it further breaks the (c) >= 90% fairness goal, so
  the tuning protects fairness. This is a limit of the fixed Rookie behavior.
- Exposure's measurable effect vs the pre-exposure baseline: stationary spammer
  82.5% -> 60%; and it lets script (1) punish an Exposed AI for 1.75x. See
  docs/benchmark-baseline.txt and docs/decisions/008-exposure-anti-spam.md.
